USB-C SuperSpeed Hub — Case C
================================
Project Introduction & Design Overview
Date: 2026-11-02
Status: Hand-solder prototype BOM & schematic review complete

1. PROJECT PURPOSE
------------------
Case C is a compact, host-powered USB-C SuperSpeed (USB 3.1 Gen1 / 5 Gbps) hub.
It provides:
  - 1× Type-C upstream (UFP) port that receives power and data from a host
  - 3× Type-C downstream (DFP) ports for devices

Key constraints:
  - No onboard MCU
  - No barrel-jack or external power supply
  - Powered solely from the host’s 5 V VBUS (typical 7.5–15 W)
  - Hand-solderable packages (SOT-23, gull-wing, no hot-bar QFN-only converters)
  - Minimal BOM cost for a quantity-1 prototype (~$56 USD)

Target use: portable USB-C hub for consumer electronics prototypes, audio devices,
or any system that needs additional SuperSpeed Type-C ports without a separate
power brick.

--------------------------

                     ▼
            ┌─────────────────┐
            │  HD3SS3220 (U2) │  ← Upstream CC + 10 Gbps SS mux (PORT = UFP)
            │  + ESD          │
            └────────┬────────┘
                     │ SuperSpeed + USB2
                     ▼
            ┌─────────────────┐
            │   TUSB8041A     │  ← 4-port USB 3.1 Gen1 hub (U1)
            │  (VQFN-64)      │
            └────────┬────────┘
         ┌───────────┼───────────┐
         ▼           ▼           ▼
   HD3SS3220     HD3SS3220     HD3SS3220   ← Downstream CC + SS mux
   (U3 DFP)      (U4 DFP)      (U5 DFP)      (PORT = DFP, 3 A advertise)
         │           │           │
         ▼           ▼           ▼
   TPS2553       TPS2553       TPS2553     ← VBUS load switches (~1 A each)
   (U6)          (U7)          (U8)
         │           │           │

Power tree (host 5 V only):
  SYS_5V ──► TLV75533PDYDR (U31, SOT-23-5 DYD) ──► 3.3 V
           └──► TLV62569PDDCR (U32, SOT-23-6) + 1 µH ──► 1.1 V
                (PG gates GRSTz of the hub)

3. KEY COMPONENTS
-----------------
Data path
  - TUSB8041ARGCR (U1)     4-port USB 3.1 Gen1 SuperSpeed hub
  - HD3SS3220RNHR ×4       Integrated CC controller + 10 Gbps 2:1 SS mux
                           (one UFP, three DFP)
  - ABM8-24.000 MHz crystal

Power management
  - TLV75533PDYDR (U31)    5 V → 3.3 V LDO, 500 mA, SOT-23-5 with thermal pad
                           (quiet rail for hub VDD33 + four HD3SS3220 VCC33)
  - TLV62569PDDCR (U32)    5 V → 1.1 V synchronous buck, 1.5 MHz, SOT-23-6
                           sized for 0.70 A (typical hub SuperSpeed load)
  - XFL4015-102MEB (L32)   1.0 µH shielded inductor
  - TPS2553DBVR ×3         Downstream VBUS switches, ~1.0 A ILIM each
  - Optional CSD17313Q2    Reverse-blocking FET on upstream 5 V

Protection & passives
  - TPD2EUSB30DRTR ×8      Low-capacitance SS ESD (2 per Type-C)
  - TPD4E05U06DQAR ×4      CC / DP / DM ESD
  - PESD5V0S1UL ×4         5 V TVS on each Type-C VBUS (also protects 5.5 V abs-max of buck)
  - 100 nF AC-coupling capacitors on all SuperSpeed TX pairs
  - Bulk capacitance: 180 µF OS-CON on SYS_5V + local 22 µF on each VBUS

Connectors
  - Amphenol 124019572112A mid-mount Type-C 24-pin, 5 A rated ×4

4. DESIGN DECISIONS & RATIONALE
-------------------------------
Hand-solder focus
  Both power converters were deliberately chosen in SOT-23 gull-wing packages
  (TLV75533PDYDR and TLV62569PDDCR) so the board can be assembled with an iron
  and flux. No WSON/VSON hot-bar parts remain on the power tree.

Why HD3SS3220 instead of a discrete TUSB542?
  The TUSB542 is a 5 Gbps redriver + 2:1 mux that still requires a separate
  CC controller to drive its SEL pin. The HD3SS3220 integrates the CC logic
  and the SuperSpeed mux in one device, eliminating the need for an extra
  redriver/mux stage on every port. This saves BOM cost, board area, and
  ~100 mA of 1.8 V current per port. A redriver would only be added later if
  measured channel loss (long traces, many vias) requires active equalization.

Power sequencing
  The 1.1 V buck’s power-good (PG) pin is used to hold the TUSB8041A GRSTz
  low until both 3.3 V and 1.1 V are stable (plus a 1 µF capacitor on GRSTz).
  This satisfies the hub’s power-on reset timing without an external MCU.

Current budget
  - 3.3 V rail load ≤ 0.20 A
  - 1.1 V rail sized for 0.70 A (covers typical 3× SuperSpeed U0 + overhead)
  - Each downstream VBUS limited to ~1.0 A by the TPS2553; three ports share
    the remaining host power budget.

5. KNOWN REVIEW ITEMS (from schematic review)
---------------------------------------------
- SuperSpeed ESD coverage: the 8× TPD2EUSB30 protect only 2 of the 4 Type-C
  SuperSpeed pairs per port. CC/DP/DM are covered by TPD4E05U06. SBU pins are
  unused and unprotected.
- PESD5V0S1UL is SOD-882 (not SOD-323). Its VBR ≥ 6.4 V, so it does not clamp
  below the 6.0 V absolute-maximum rating of the TLV62569 / TLV75533.
- 5 V-only system: a CC short-to-VBUS is 5 V, not 20 V.
- TUSB8041 datasheet recommends a VBUS ferrite + 100 nF; confirm this is
  present on the upstream port.
- Pins on the schematic are functional labels — always verify against the
  official datasheets before releasing a PCB.

7. BRING-UP NOTES (HIGH LEVEL)
------------------------------
1. Visual + continuity check (no power) — especially QFN thermal pads and
   differential pairs.
2. Current-limited 5 V apply; verify 3.3 V and 1.1 V rails and GRSTz timing.
3. Connect upstream to a USB 3.x host; confirm both “Generic USB SuperSpeed
   Hub” and “Generic USB 2.0 Hub” appear.
4. Test each downstream port with a known SuperSpeed device in both cable
   orientations.
5. Only then move to targeted signal checks (USB 2.0 chirp/SOF, SuperSpeed
   LFPS activity) if functional test fails.

8. SUMMARY
----------
Case C is a minimal, host-powered, hand-solderable 1-to-3 USB-C SuperSpeed hub
built around the TUSB8041A and four HD3SS3220 devices. Power conversion uses
SOT-23 LDO and buck converters so the board can be assembled without hot-air
or hot-bar equipment. The design prioritizes low BOM cost, correct Type-C
orientation handling, and reliable power sequencing for a quantity-1 prototype.

End of introduction.
